When a loop will not end

Module 02 · Control flow — Lesson 8 of 18

Every loop is a bet. You are betting that the condition you wrote will eventually become false, and the machine will hold you to it: if the bet is wrong, the program does not crash, it simply never comes back. That is the difference between a bug you can read in a stack trace and a bug you have to reason about.

There are three ways a loop ends. The counter reaches its bound, the collection runs out of items, or something inside the body changes the condition. If you cannot point at which of the three applies to a loop you have written, you have not finished writing it.

Look at the shape below. The condition tests total < 100, and the body adds step to total on every pass — so it terminates for any positive step. Set step = 0 and the same three lines run forever, because nothing in the body moves the condition.

counting.py 
# terminates for any step > 0
total = 0
step  = 7

while total < 100:
    total = total + step
    print(total)

The habit worth building is to name the quantity that shrinks. Here it is 100 - total: it gets smaller by step every pass and cannot go below zero forever, so the loop must stop. A quantity like that is called a variant, and a loop that has one is a loop you can defend.

When you are stuck in a lab and something has hung, the first question is not "what is wrong with my code" but "what was supposed to be shrinking, and is it". Nine times in ten the answer is that the line meant to shrink it sits outside the loop body, one indent too far left.
