Question 20 of 22 · version 1 · 14 marks

Model answer

Compare an adjacency list with an adjacency matrix for a sparse graph, in space and in traversal cost. Justify which you would choose for a road network of 40,000 junctions, and say what would change your mind.

Answer given

An adjacency matrix stores a cell for every pair of vertices, so it costs Θ(V²) whether or not the edges exist. A list stores one entry per edge, so it costs Θ(V + E). A road network is sparse — a junction meets four or five roads, not forty thousand — so E is roughly linear in V and the list is smaller by about a factor of V. For 40,000 junctions the matrix needs 1.6 billion cells and the list needs a couple of hundred thousand entries.

Traversal follows the same shape. A breadth-first search over a list touches each edge once and runs in O(V + E); over a matrix it scans a whole row per vertex and runs in O(V²), so it does most of its work reading zeroes.

I would choose the list. What would change my mind is a dense graph, or a workload dominated by hasEdge(u, v) lookups — the matrix answers that in constant time while the list scans a neighbour list.
