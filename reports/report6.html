Everyday learning

Onyx Learn

Courses, lessons, attendance and homework — the part every learner touches daily.

01  ·  Delivered

Browse and join courses

Every course the institution offers, in one place. Depending on how a course is set up a learner joins it instantly, pays to unlock it, or is added by the institution — all three routes were used during this check.

What a learner sees: their courses, and the full catalogue  /onyx/courses
02  ·  Delivered

Video, reading and document lessons

A course can mix video, documents, images, links and written pages. Every kind of lesson — not just video — remembers exactly where a learner stopped, so moving from a phone to a laptop carries on from the same line.

A real lesson, part-finished, with progress kept automatically  /onyx/courses/626/lessons/466
03  ·  Delivered

Attendance, by QR code or by hand

The teacher opens a session and a code goes on the projector. Learners scan it with their own phone — no app. The code redraws itself every 15 seconds, so a photograph sent to a friend outside the room is dead within half a minute. The register can also be marked by hand, and every learner carries a running percentage.

A live session: rotating code on the left, the register filling on the right  /onyx/courses/593/attendance/601
04  ·  Delivered

Homework: set it, mark it, hand it back

Work is set with its marking guide attached, handed in online, and comes back with a score and written feedback. Late work is handled by the institution's own rule rather than by the teacher remembering it.

Real work, handed in, marked against real criteria  /onyx/assignments/119
05  ·  Delivered

One screen: what to do next

The learner's home screen answers one question — what should I do right now. The course they were last in, the work that is due, a streak counted from real activity, and a readiness score that says out loud which single thing would raise it fastest.

A learner's own dashboard, with its reasoning on show  /onyx/dashboard
06  ·  Delivered

Ask a question, get a real answer

Questions are asked on the course itself, where the people teaching it can see them. If one goes unanswered it becomes a support ticket with a clock on it, so a real problem is never left sitting in a thread.

The help queue, and how the product explains its own process  /onyx/support
Learning to code

Onyx Code Lab

A real editor, real execution and instant marking, with nothing to install.

07  ·  Delivered

Write and run real code, in the browser

Nothing to install. A learner writes code in the same kind of editor a professional developer uses, presses Run, and sees what their program actually printed.

The editor, open on a real problem, with its constraints and examples  /onyx/practice/125
08  ·  Delivered

Code runs safely, every time

Every run happens inside a locked-down space that cannot reach the internet, cannot take more than its share of the machine, and cannot touch anybody else's work. Confirmed the only way worth confirming it: by attacking it.

Real code, really executed, with the real answer coming back  /onyx/practice/125
09  ·  Delivered

Marked automatically, the instant it runs

Work is checked against prepared cases — some shown to the learner, some kept hidden — with partial credit for partly right, and feedback that names what happened rather than just passing or failing.

One of one cases passed, in 11 milliseconds  /onyx/practice/125
10  ·  Delivered

A library of practice problems

Organised by difficulty and by topic, with hints that cost something to reveal and worked solutions that stay locked until a learner has earned them.

The bank, filtered by difficulty and by topic  /onyx/practice
11  ·  Delivered

Real, multi-file coding projects

Past single exercises: a project with several files, a save-point button so an earlier state is never lost, and the option to hand it to the teacher of that course for review.

A learner's own project space  /onyx/workspaces
Tests and exams

Onyx Assess

Scheduled papers, watched exams, marking that a human still owns.

12  ·  Delivered

Scheduled, timed online tests

Papers open and close on the server's clock, not the learner's, and are drawn from randomised banks so a room full of people does not sit one identical paper.

A learner's papers: open now, closed, or still being marked  /onyx/assessments
13  ·  Delivered

Exam integrity, watched and then judged by a person

Where an exam calls for it, camera and screen are watched and anything odd is flagged. The console orders attempts worst-first — and a flag is evidence for a human to weigh, never an automatic fail.

The invigilation console, saying plainly what a flag is and is not  /onyx/invigilate
14  ·  Delivered

Marking: automatic where it can be, by hand where it matters

Objective questions are marked the moment they are submitted. Written answers go to a person with the marking guide in front of them, with a second pair of eyes available before anything is published.

A marking queue, with real papers waiting on a real person  /onyx/assessments
15  ·  Delivered

Results, the moment they are released

A learner sees their score, their grade and how the cohort did, in one place — and sees none of it until the institution releases it, however long the marking takes.

One learner's released results, and nothing that is not released  /onyx/results
Getting a job

Onyx Career

Contests, interviews, verifiable certificates and the employers on the other side.

16  ·  Delivered

Live coding contests

A timed contest with a leaderboard, teams, penalty minutes and a frozen board near the finish — judged by the same sandbox that marks everyday practice.

A contest running live, with the board judging as it goes  /onyx/contests
17  ·  Delivered

Practice job interviews

Booked through the placement office, scored against written criteria, recorded only with the learner's consent. The learner sees the score and the comments; the interviewer's private notes stay private.

The learner's side  /onyx/interviews
The same feature, from the employer's side  /onyx/interviews
18  ·  Delivered

Every credential carries its own id. Anyone holding that id can open a public page — no account, no login — and see whether it is genuine, who issued it, and whether it was ever withdrawn.

The institution's register, one certificate freshly issued  /onyx/certificates
The same credential, seen by a stranger with no account  /onyx/verify/BB173F56F69D8F039AB1678787ACC09C
19  ·  Delivered

Jobs, applications and employers

Employers post roles, learners apply against stated eligibility rules, and the placement office runs the pipeline. Each employer sees exactly their own applicants — and no other company's.

A learner's applications  /onyx/jobs
The employer's own posts  /onyx/jobs
20  ·  Delivered

A profile built to be sent to an employer

A shareable profile where every skill traces back to real evidence, plus a resume builder that assembles an A4 PDF from the learner's record at the moment they press the button — so it can never be out of date.

The profile, and what it says is still missing  /onyx/profile

Onyx Campus

Programmes, examinations, money, parents and the security under all of it.

21  ·  Delivered

Programmes, terms and timetables

Programmes, terms, cohorts, who teaches what and when — set up and published from one console, with a double-booked room or teacher refused before it ever reaches a learner's timetable.

A programme with its term and its cohort, set up live during this check  /onyx/programs
22  ·  Delivered

Examinations, start to finish

Scheduling, seating, marks entry, moderation and publication — with the candidate's script and the marker's copy both downloadable as PDFs.

The examinations office's own calendar, mid-term  /onyx/exams

Fees, payments and receipts

Fee structures, invoices, online payment, receipts and who still owes what — with course and live-class income tracked apart from fee income so the books never blur.

24  ·  Delivered

Parents, only where the learner says so

A guardian signs in and sees attendance, results and fees — but only after the learner invites them, and only the parts the learner switched on. The learner can withdraw any of it at any time.

What a linked guardian sees, and what they are told they cannot  /onyx/family
25  ·  Delivered

Secure by design, not by promise

One institution's data is unreachable from another's. Every role sees only what it is allowed to, written in plain words rather than buried in a config file. Every consequential action is written to a record nobody can edit afterwards. 140 deliberate attempts to read another institution's data — none succeeded.

What each role may do, editable per institution  /onyx/permissions
The permanent record: who, what, when, and from where  /onyx/audit
Part four

The extra features, one by one

The same twenty-one, described properly. Where one has a screen of its own, it is here.

For your learners

3 extra features
Extra 01

A resume builder, and a profile worth sharing

A learner assembles an A4 resume from their own record in one click. It is rebuilt from live data every time, so it cannot go stale, and they can switch on a public profile page to send an employer.

Built from the record itself, not a stored copy  /onyx/resume
Extra 02

A readiness score that explains itself

Not just a number out of a hundred. The points still unearned are broken out and ranked, so a learner is told which single thing is worth the most to them right now.

The score, with its own reasoning beside it  /onyx/dashboard
Extra 03

Web-page problems, not only code problems

Alongside ordinary exercises a learner can be set an HTML, CSS and JavaScript build with a live preview of the page they are making, marked by a person who sees the rendered result.

For teaching

3 extra features
Extra 04

A check-in code that defeats proxy attendance

The proposal asked for a QR code or a paper register. What exists rotates the code every fifteen seconds and accepts it for its own cycle and the next only, so a photograph sent to a friend outside the room is useless within half a minute.

The code on the projector, redrawing itself  /onyx/courses/593/attendance/601
Extra 05

Questions that get closed, not just answered

The learner who asked marks the reply that actually answered them, and useful answers are voted up. Anything still unanswered becomes a ticket with a deadline and an owner's name on it.

Extra 06

Cohorts can be retired, not only deleted

A section that has moved on is closed so it stops appearing everywhere, without destroying the record of who was in it.

For examinations

4 extra features
Extra 07

Parallel papers, rotated down the register

The proposal asked for randomised banks. A bank here holds whole parallel papers, and a scheduled examination hands them out by roll number, so nobody within sight of a neighbour is sitting the same paper.

Extra 08

Anonymous marking, and a gate before publication

A marker can be shown Candidate 1 rather than a name, and results can be held back until every attempt has been past a second pair of eyes.

Extra 09

Seating plans, and every script downloadable

Halls are filled in seat order, and a candidate's script can be downloaded on its own or for the whole sitting at once.

Extra 10

Integrity flags a named person decides on

Events carry weights and roll into a score banded high, medium and low, ordered worst first. Each is dismissed or upheld by a named person, the decision is kept, and the candidate can see the outcome.

Ordered worst-first, waiting on a human decision  /onyx/invigilate

For credentials

2 extra features
Extra 11

A certificate that keeps answering after it is withdrawn

A revoked credential is never deleted. Its public page keeps resolving and says the credential was withdrawn — the only answer of any use to whoever is holding a copy of it.

In force and withdrawn, both kept on the register  /onyx/certificates
Extra 12

Verification with no account at all

Whoever is checking a certificate signs up for nothing. They open the page and it tells them what the issuer published, and nothing else.

What a stranger sees, signed out  /onyx/verify/BB173F56...

For selling courses

3 extra features
Extra 13

Live Classes — paid cohort programmes

Four-, twelve- and sixteen-week live programmes with their own pricing, paid registration and a named certificate at the end.

Extra 14

Courses sold one at a time

A locked course carries a price and is bought before it opens, and that revenue is reported apart from institutional fee income.

Course income, kept separate from fees  /onyx/finance
Extra 15

A complete public storefront

A marketing site with its own catalogue and price filters, workshops, instructor pages, a knowledge base, cart, checkout, coupons, a wishlist, purchase history and invoices — effectively a second application beside the institutional one.

For running the platform

6 extra features
Extra 16

An operator console above every institution

Create, suspend or remove an entire institution — and, where a customer wants it, run theirs for them: their students, staff, courses, timetable, fees and help queue, without them ever signing in.

Every institution on the platform  /onyx/platform
One of them, run from above  /onyx/platform/tenants/798
Extra 17

Each institution's settings in its own hands

Whether learners may register themselves, which email domains are accepted, the community link on the jobs page — changed by the institution, not by a support request.

An institution's own controls  /onyx/settings
Extra 18

A permission matrix anyone can read

Ten areas of permission, editable for every role, written as plain sentences — with a reset to defaults, and wording that explains a tick grants the act, not the reach.

Every role against every permission  /onyx/permissions
Extra 19

A record of what the platform team does

Separate from each institution's own log: every change an operator makes anywhere, and the privileged reads too — opening somebody's grades is recorded against the operator's name.

The operator's own record  /onyx/platform/audit
Extra 20

Other software can connect, with permission

An outside application can ask a signed-in user for permission to act on their behalf, and that permission can be taken back at any time.

Extra 21

A dark theme across the whole product

Every screen, not a subset, and checked for contrast in both themes.

In closing

Forty-six features, and a product you can open right now.

Twenty-five were promised in the proposal and twenty-five are running. Twenty-one more were built on top of them, and every one of those is something a learner, a lecturer, an examinations officer or your own operations team can use — not an internal engineering detail.

Every screen in this document was opened, used and photographed in the live product while it was being written: a learner sat a practice problem and it was graded, a lecturer opened a register and the check-in code rotated on screen, a certificate was issued and then verified by somebody with no account at all.

What is left is your content, your people and your calendar — not more software.
